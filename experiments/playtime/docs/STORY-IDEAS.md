# Story ideas — tales, rhymes and nature topics as Mipo spikes

status: idea

Started 2026-11-16 from Childcraft (the 1970s volumes Nikki owns, `INSPIRATIONS.md`). The volume titles here
are from memory: check them against the shelf. Nikki: "i like all these ideas". Folk tales, fables and nursery
rhymes are public domain. Each idea gets a verb (what the kid does) and the spikes it would build on. These are
ideas to pick from, not a plan. A picked one becomes a spike (run `./playtime.sh tried <word>` first) or a
candidate for app one (`APP-ONE.md` AO1).

## Folk tales and fables (Folk and Fairy Tales, Stories and Fables)

- **The Enormous Turnip** — *pull.* Mipos grab each other into a chain and pull; the joints stretch and
  wobble until *pop*, everyone tumbles. Pure physics, very little to build. reach, ragdoll.
- **Three Billy Goats Gruff** — *cross.* A wobbly plank bridge, a troll underneath who bumps it. planks.
- **The Three Little Pigs** — *build, blow.* A house of straw, sticks or Fabuland blocks, then the wind
  blows: which one stands? fabuland, weather.
- **The Gingerbread Man** — *bake, chase.* Decorate the dough like Puppetmaker; out of the oven it runs away.
  maker, food.
- **Stone Soup** — *add.* A big pot every Mipo throws something into: bubbles, a colour change, a bowl each.
  food, liquid, waterjar.
- **The Mitten** (Ukrainian) — *squeeze in.* Animals squeeze one by one into a mitten that stretches, until a
  mouse sneezes and *pop*. soft torso, blob.
- **The Little Red Hen** — *sow, grow, bake.* Grain to flour to bread. plant-grow, food.
- **Henny Penny** — *panic.* Something falls on a head, everyone runs. emotions.
- **The Tortoise and the Hare** — *race.* Two breeds on a track. tracks, coaster, breeds.
- **The Lion and the Mouse** — *free.* A net or a knot a small Mipo gnaws through. cloth, rope.
- **Town Mouse and Country Mouse** — two worlds side by side, burrow and house. burrow, depth. (The
  underground-Mipos hunch of 2026-11-01.)
- **The Elves and the Shoemaker** — *make at night.* You make shoes; in the morning the Mipos wear them.
  wearables, `SHOES.md`.

## Nursery rhymes (Poems and Rhymes)

- **Humpty Dumpty** — *fall, put back together.* Off the wall in pieces, then snap the parts back on. The
  Mipo ragdoll is already the toy.
- **Rub-a-dub-dub, three men in a tub** — *sail.* A tub with three Mipos in it. boat2, drawn-sea.
- **Hickory Dickory Dock** — *climb.* A big clock with a pendulum, a mouse runs up, *bong*, it runs down.
  music, synth.
- **The Old Woman Who Lived in a Shoe** — a shoe as a dollhouse, full of Mipos. dollhouse.
- **Jack and Jill** — *carry, roll.* A bucket of water up the hill, then down it. waterjar, a slope.
- **Hey Diddle Diddle** — *jump.* The cow over the moon: a trampoline arc, the moon in the sky. clouds.
- **Row, Row, Row Your Boat** — *row,* sung as a round. choir, boat.

## Nature and science (About Animals, The Green Kingdom, World and Space, How Things Change, Science All Around)

- **Underground** — moles and worms in their tunnels. burrow.
- **The seasons** — one tree through the year: leaves fall, snow stays. tree-grow, weather.
- **Caterpillar to butterfly** — *feed,* a cocoon, the surprise. A reveal like the bathhouse's.
- **The water cycle** — cloud, rain, river, sea, steam. clouds, sand (its steam rains back down).
- **Nests and eggs** — *bring sticks,* a bird builds a nest, an egg hatches a baby Mipo.
- **Day and night** — *turn* a dial; the Mipos go to bed. light.
- **Floats or sinks?** — *throw* things in the water, the encyclopedia experiment. The sand spike's buoyancy
  already does it.
- **Simple machines** — a seesaw, a crane, a pulley with a bucket. joints, motors.

## Make and Do, Children Everywhere

- **Paper crafts** — *cut, fold, glue,* and the drawing comes alive. cutpaper.
- **Puppet theatre** — the curtain opens, the kid plays the story with Mipos. Puppetmaker's natural sequel.
- **The kitchen** — Mipos baking cookies. food, an oven.
- **Houses of the world** — stilt houses, igloos, windmills. fabuland parts per style.

## Cutaways (2026-11-16)

Nikki, after the whale: "that cutaway concept, can we do more with that?" Picture books are full of it
(Childcraft's cross-sections, Richard Scarry's houses with the front off, Stephen Biesty's *Incredible
Cross-Sections*, an ant farm); the dollhouse, the burrow and the whale already are.

- **Peel the front off** — closed from outside; a swipe folds the skin open like a flap and shows who's
  inside, closed again it's quiet. Peekaboo. Tried first, on the whale (2026-11-16).
- **See-through only where someone is** — the skin turns see-through around a Mipo that walks in.
- **An x-ray glass** — drag a magnifying glass over an animal, a house, a tree, a suitcase.
- **More sleeping animals** — a bear in a cave, a cow, a dragon: the whale's data table, each with its own way
  of sending Mipos out (the bear snores them out, the cow moos).
- **A tree from inside** — squirrel rooms in the trunk, roots underground, an owl at the top. bark, trees,
  burrow.
- **A ship in cross-section** — cabins, the hold, the engine room, the galley, the sea outside. boat2,
  drawn-sea.
- **A house with the front off** — Fabuland/Busytown floors, stairs and a lift. fabuland, depth layers.
- **A submarine or diving bell**, **an anthill or a beehive.**
- **How it works inside** — a clock (gears, a Mipo riding one, Hickory Dickory Dock), a windmill (sails turn
  the stone, flour out at the bottom; the Little Red Hen), a tummy (food in, through and out,
  `FOOD-IN-FOOD-OUT.md`).
- **In the engine:** they all share a shell drawn over the Mipos, a back wall behind them, and floors. One
  shared cutaway piece only after a second cutaway spike, when there are two copies to compare.

## Book mechanics: flaps, tabs, wheels (2026-11-16)

Nikki: "in children's books we have more flaps or pieces to pull usually". Many get better with a touchscreen
and physics.

- **Lift the flap** (*Where's Spot?*, *Dear Zoo*) — many small flaps: a door, a cupboard, a rug, a bush; under
  each a Mipo saying "boo", or a thing. Peekaboo twenty times.
- **Peep inside** (Usborne *Peep Inside*) — the cutaway with flaps in it: open the whale, then smaller flaps
  inside (the stomach, a cupboard in the cabin). Flaps inside flaps.
- **Pull tab** (Meggendorfer's moving books, *Spot*) — pull a tab and something moves: a tail wags, a mouth
  opens, a Mipo pops out of the chimney. With physics the tab is a slider on a joint: pull slowly or hard.
- **Turning wheel / volvelle** (*Ladybird* and other teaching books) — turn a disc: day and night, the seasons,
  the weather, what a Mipo wears. Fits "day and night" above.
- **Pop-up** (Jan Pieńkowski's *Haunted House*, Robert Sabuda) — open the page and a house or a tree folds up
  out of the ground; Fabuland parts unfolding.
- **Mix-and-match strips** (*Mixed-Up Animals*, flip books) — head, body and legs on strips you flip. That's
  Puppetmaker; as book pages a fun variant.
- **Holes in the page** (*The Very Hungry Caterpillar*) — a piece seen through a hole that turns out to be
  something else on the next page; a Mipo peeking through a hole in a wall.
- **Touch and feel** (*That's Not My…*) — fur, felt, sandpaper (the materials spikes); on a screen, feel by
  sound: each material its own rubbing sound.
- **Gatefold** — a page that folds out into a long panorama: the whale getting longer and longer, a train.
- **Press here** (Hervé Tullet's *Press Here*) — tap a dot and something happens; tilt the phone and
  everything rolls. Tilt is gravity, so it suits physics very well: one verb (tilt) that sand, floats and the
  whale all fit under.
- **In the engine:** the whale's peel is the first flap, built as a small piece of its own (a hinge line, a
  shape, how open it is) so more flaps are cheap. Pull tabs and wheels are joints with a motor or a slider.

## Real toys and crafts (2026-11-16)

Nikki: "getting inspired by other real world children's crafting toys or games ... what other layers can we
spike out".

- **Marionettes** — strings from a crossbar the finger moves; Mipos are physics puppets already. The closest
  step from Puppetmaker.
- **Shadow puppets** — a lamp behind a sheet, Mipos as silhouettes (light, shadows in the engine).
- **Stickers, Colorforms, Fuzzy Felt** — peel a sticker off a sheet and stick it on: a hat on a Mipo, a sun in
  the sky; felt sticks a little and pulls off again.
- **Playdough and clay** — knead and roll shapes that become a body (soft torso, blob).
- **Stamps, potato prints** — press and it prints; Mipo feet leave prints (`paint-marks.lua`).
- **Marble run** — build a track, roll balls or Mipos through (soapbox, coaster).
- **Snow globe** — shake it and the snow swirls round a Mipo inside; sand, glitter, bubbles.
- **Music box with a paper strip** — punch holes in a strip, turn the crank, a tune; the Mipos dance or sing
  along (synth, choir).
- **Jack-in-the-box, wind-up toys** — wind the crank, the tension builds, pop; wind up a Mipo and it waddles
  off (motor). Spiked first (2026-11-16). A wind-up car was tried and parked with the rides (`RIDES.md`).
- **Nesting dolls** — a Mipo in a Mipo in a Mipo: the whale, small and endless.
- **Zoetrope, flip book** — spin the drum and the drawings move.
- **Kaleidoscope, spirograph** — turn it and a drawing becomes a pattern; quiet play.

## What a book can't do (2026-11-16)

Nikki: "we can do things you cannot in a book".

- **Blow on the screen** (the microphone) — blow out candles, a dandelion, wind in a Mipo's sail, a soap
  bubble. Very physical for small kids; one verb, an app of its own.
- **Your own drawing comes alive** (the camera) — draw a fish on paper, photograph it, it swims in the whale.
  The scan-to-texture tool (`ASSETS-AND-BUILDS.md` AB1).
- **The Mipos hear you** — say something and a Mipo says it back in Mipo language (mipomi-lang, voicebox).
- **Zoom into a flap** — behind a flap another world with more flaps, on and on (Istvan Banyai's *Zoom*).
- **It remembers** — the Mipo put under the bush is still there tomorrow; a plant sown yesterday has grown.
- **Real time** — dark outside, night in the book, the Mipos asleep.
- **Off the page** — a Mipo walks off the right edge and turns up on the next page.
- **Consequences** — paper gets wet, dirty, burns, tears (sand, dirt, floats); in a book drawn water stays
  drawn.
- **Two kids at once** — multitouch: two siblings each pull an end of the turnip.
- **Tying things together with ropes** (Nikki: "connecting things with ropes is also a thing kids like") — tie
  a Mipo to a balloon, a cart to a cart into a train, a bucket on a rope, a swing. `DOLL-PLAY-MENU.md` has
  the rope bridge, clothesline, tow rope and bucket already.

Picks to spike first: blow on the screen, marionettes, your own drawing in the scene.

## Built as spikes (2026-11-16)

The four quickest, each its own scene in `scripts/spikes/`, not yet played by Nikki by hand:

- **turnip** — a chain of Mipos pulls; a Mipo dropped behind the last one grabs on with both hands. One or two
  can't, three can (the count decides: Mipos in the chain + 0.7 for the kid's drag must reach `need` 2.8;
  measured through the chain the pull depended on how well the random Mipos' feet gripped). Pop: a kick, a
  camera shake, everyone tumbles, the turnip lies on the ground (Nikki: "when pulled should be on top of the
  ground"). Open: the chain never lets go after the pop; the turnip and leaves are plain shapes.
- **humpty** — a pear-bodied Mipo (rubberleg-pearbod) on a brick wall. A hard landing breaks the shoulder and
  hip joints (recorded first); drag a piece within 170 of its pulsing socket and it clicks back on (the
  engine's createJoint, same id). Open: a grey bar on loose limbs; `humpty-joints.lua` exists only because the
  script env has no `joints`.
- **mitten** — ~100 wool beads on springs, each pulled to its place in the mitten's shape, open at the cuff.
  Five inside: "ah… ah… tchoo!", the mitten puffs, everyone flies out, it springs back. Open: the cuff (~600)
  is lower than a Mipo (~660); the bulge barely shows; the camera starts too far left.
- **floats** — a glass tub (`props.makeBath`, the engine's level water) and 11 things on a table: ball, cork,
  duck, block, apple and ice float; egg sinks slowly, the sponge soaks and sinks, stone, key and coin drop.
  Lift by displaced water per fixture (the engine's floatIn lifts by mass, so everything floated alike), a
  splash and bubbles, a bloop. Open: the table and tub frame are plain grey; a Mipo can pin the cork.

- **belly** — the enormous animal (Nikki: "the mitten inspired me ... an enormous animal laying on the ground and
  mipos can walk inside the animal (a bit like the whale)"). A sleeping whale on a beach as a picture-book
  cutaway: Mipos walk in by themselves up the tongue (4.7°), a mouth cave with baleen, a stomach with ribs
  behind a narrow throat. Its back is the mitten's springy beads; a 6 s breath swells it and rocks the floor.
  Six inside: a shiver, then a hiccup (the jaw and eye open, a spout, "hup") throws them all onto the beach. The
  animal is a data table (outline, floor, jaw, blowhole), so a bear or a cow is another table. Open: big Mipos
  stop at the throat; the rocking is subtle; plain shapes, wants a drawing.
  **Peel** (2026-11-16, Nikki: "do the peel spike on the whale"): closed, the skin hides everyone inside; drag
  the flank down (or tap, or `o`) and it folds toward you like a page, head end first in a curl, past halfway
  it finishes; the whale twitches and peeks with one eye. Inside, a second flap: a pouch in the stomach wall
  with a sleeping fish (flaps inside flaps). The flap is its own small piece in the script (a def: hinge,
  colours, curl; state on `state`), so a new flap is one def. Open: drag and tap not yet tried by hand; Mipos
  can be grabbed through the closed skin; no muffled voices (no volume hook for scripts).

- **book** — the book mechanics as a Mipo picture book (Nikki: "shall we do a spike with all sorts of flaps,
  pulls, wheels and the children book stuff?"; "looks pretty promising"). An open book on a table, real
  Mipos on the pages, pages turned by dragging a corner (the turn is a big flap). Spread 1 lift the flap (door,
  windows, bush, mailbox; a Mipo, a cat, a cake, a sock under them), 2 a pull tab (a Mipo rides up the
  chimney) and a wheel behind a window (day to night), 3 a pop-up tree and house with Mipos on them, 4 press
  here dots and tilt (arrow keys tip gravity). The flap is now `scripts/spikes/book-flaps.lua`, shared with
  belly. Open: the next spread is blank under a turning page; Mipos are rebuilt every turn; Mipos on the
  pop-up drop when it folds fast; tilt moves gravity, not the picture; nothing tried by hand or on the phone.

- **windup** — parked (tried 2026-11-16; Nikki: "lets park this winding up", moving on to an activity board).
  A key on anything (`scripts/spikes/windup-key.lua`): turn it round to wind (ratchet clicks, sproing when
  overwound), the spring hands its power 0..1 to the toy. On a table: Mipo waddler, hopper and drummer
  (crouch and shiver when wound, a clockwork stutter, walk off the edge), a car on motor wheels, a spinning
  top that wobbles and falls, a jack-in-the-box (a note per click of Pop Goes the Weasel, the clown pops on
  the last), a music box slowing down. Open when picked up again: the toys are small next to the Mipos; Mipos
  off the table leave the view; nothing tried by hand.

- **beadmaze** — a bead maze (Nikki: "could we do a bead maze next, like beads on rails?"). A wooden base, five
  painted wires (a hump, a loop, a zigzag, a figure-8, a low arc crossing the rest, in front of or behind the
  Mipos), 23 beads (round, cube, cylinder, flower). A bead is a distance along its wire and a speed: gravity
  along the slope, a drag and a rub so it settles in dips, a knock at the ends; beads on a wire clack and pass
  speed on by weight. Drag a bead along its wire, space shakes the toy. Not rail-cart (that lets go at the
  ends; a bead never does). Open: dragging not tried by hand; a hit on a resting stack barely bounces; no
  Mipo riding a bead, no drawing your own wire.

- **activity, activity-vintage** — two activity boards (Nikki: "like the Fisher-Price vintage activity center?
  lets spike that"; "can we have both the boards?"). Original Mipo boards, no brand or copied artwork. The
  first: a red-rimmed board with a dial, a picture wheel, a bell, a bead drum, a peekaboo door with a Mipo, a
  squeaker, beads on a rod, a clicker knob and a pull string (a rope) that zips back while the Mipos sing. The
  vintage one, laid out from Nikki's photo of a 1970s activity center (described by the other session): ivory
  plastic in relief (a vine, the egg Mipo on a wall), a button that pushes a plunger up to the bell, a
  picture window with a knob (cow over the moon, egg Mipo, whale, mitten), a spinning ball, a roller, a lever
  that sails three Mipos in a tub, a MIPO name plate, the dial, a rainbow spinner, a squeaker door, a mirror,
  a turtle and bunny race along the bottom. The shared gadgets and sounds are in
  `scripts/spikes/activity-gadgets.lua`. The Mipos look at what made a sound, jump at the bell, sing with the
  string. Open: nothing tried by hand; Mipos can be grabbed through the closed door; panels overlap a gadget.

- **pushpuppet** — parked (tried 2026-11-16; Nikki: "good enough for this spike for now, park"). The thumb-press
  push puppet (Nikki: "help me with some other toys, see what we can mimic"; "y push puppet"): a random Mipo
  with its feet welded on a painted drum; hold the drum (or space) and it crumples, let go and it springs up;
  drag sideways (arrows) to slacken one side. Its own muscles off (ragdoll, walk held); the strings are a
  motor per revolute joint pulling to the built pose, as strong as the weight the joint holds x its lever
  (one strength for all yanked the hands and it shook without end; the hands dangle now). Pressed: no pull,
  the joint limits widened (with them its legs braced and only the head drooped; fully off it folded over the
  drum's edge), and the slack strings drag torso and head to the middle of the drum top so it slumps on it.
  Open: a joint can stay ~25° off after a release; a tilt can swing a shoulder right round; some Mipos still
  sway a little. Other vintage toys listed then, not built: jumping jack, roly-poly, ladder tumbler, pecking
  chickens, woodpecker pole, Newton's cradle, pound-a-peg, corn popper, magnetic fishing.

- **peckers** — parked (tried 2026-11-16; Nikki: "commit and park this game"). The pecking chickens paddle:
  a wooden paddle with four painted hens facing out, a ball hanging under it; drag anywhere and the paddle
  follows (kinematic), `a` swings it in circles by itself. The ball hangs on a rope joint from the middle of
  the board; each hen's string runs head -> hole -> ball, and the string pulled through on the ball's side
  (past a little give) drives that head's neck motor down: the hens away from the ball peck, with a wooden
  tock; a run of pecks makes the two watching Mipos squeak. Tried first: each string a stiff spring between
  head and ball by applyForce; it blew up (the heads too light for a spring that holds the ball). Open: the
  heads don't pull back on the ball; side on it reads left/right more than round and round; not tried by hand.

- **fishing** — parked (tried 2026-11-16; Nikki: "magnetic fsihing !", played it, then "commit and park this
  game"). The magnetic fishing game: a yellow drum pond seen a little from above, eight numbered fish standing on
  a ring (an ellipse, back ones drawn first), turning; a cam opens and shuts each mouth twice a turn. The rod tip
  follows the finger (kinematic), the magnet is a real body on a rope joint, so it swings and lags; near an open
  mouth it clicks on: the fish turns dynamic, welded by its mouth, and dangles (one swung out onto a Mipo's
  head). Lowered into the bucket it drops in, plop, the count goes up and the two watching Mipos cheer; `x`
  lets go anywhere, `r` puts all the fish back. Sounds made in the script (click, plop). Open: the magnet can
  catch on the bucket's rim; the fish on the ring are drawn, not in the water (no bob); one rod, no Mipo
  fishing.

- **woodpecker** — parked (tried 2026-11-16; Nikki: "woodpecker pole !", played it, then "commit and park").
  The woodpecker pole, all honest Box2D: the ring is two pads either side of the pole with 6 px of play (tipped,
  they pinch the pole by friction); the spring is a hinge plus a spring torque of the script's own, on the bird
  one way and the ring the other (that tips the ring); the bird leans a little head-down so each forward bob
  puts the beak on the pole. Flick it (tap, space) and it stick-slips down, ~60 px/s, two pecks a second (counted
  from Box2D's beak/pole contacts), a tock each. Tried first: a soft weld as the spring; its turning stiffness
  goes by the lighter body (the little ring) and the bird hung 60°. With the beak resting on the pole the bird
  was propped and stuck. Open: the bird is small next to the Mipos; nothing happens at the bottom.

- **watergarden** — parked (tried 2026-11-16; Nikki: "a sort of toy i dont know the name, with it involved water
  and a little plastic castle, flowers grew", picked "pour water, things grow"; "lets make the mipo wet with it
  too"). A pastel plastic castle in a garden: hold the jug to pour (drops as points, not Box2D), the funnel on the
  middle tower takes water in and two fish spouts let it out onto the beds, roofs shed it; soil cells get wetter
  (darker) and dry; seeds in wet soil grow stem, leaves, bud, then open with a chime (tulip, daisy, sunflower,
  bell, rose); dip the jug in the pond to fill it. A drop on a Mipo leaves a wet spot (dirt-look's new water
  spots, `state.dirtSpots`) and a surprised squeak; the Mipos cheer a bloom. Open: a jug empties in ~8 s; the
  crenellations read as steps; the panel covers the left bed.

- **wasserbahn** — parked (tried 2026-11-16; Nikki: "a few more toys ideas, catapult, wasserbahn"; "ok good
  enough"). A water play track: a tower lets water onto three blue gutters zigzagging down to a pool; drops slide
  along a gutter (gravity along its slope, drag), fall off its low end through a water wheel's paddles (it spins,
  ticks) onto the next. A closed lock holds a pond: level on top, its length from how much is held (n drops x area
  = L^2 sin / 2), spilling over a full board; a boat in it floats up; open it and it all rushes on (first try: the
  held drops queued in a line all the way back up). Three boats ride the water, stall where it's dry, splash into
  the pool (the Mipos cheer); tap one there to put it back at the top. A hand pump at the pool lifts water back to
  the tower a stroke at a time. Open: drops read as beads more than a stream; the pump takes many strokes; the
  catapult (asked with it) is already rooms/catapult, not redone.
