# Music & sound ideas

Notes to pick up now and then, with no deadline. The music lives in `src/music.lua` and the patches in `src/synth.lua`. The engine is dreamengine, through `native/desound/`.

## Where it is now
- **Planned, not rolled** (2026-10-28), after dreamengine's lofi.c (its `docs/design/radio-arranger-lessons.md` is the why). Each song gets a form (intro, A, A, B, A, outro, or intro, A, B, A, B, outro), an A and a B progression (B is its own move or A with a ii-V turnaround) and a comping taste. Every bar is planned knowing the next: the push (the next chord comped early), fills into B, the bass letting go when the next chord comes in early.
- **A tune that comes back.** The melody is seeded by its place in the form, so every A sings the same tune and B its own. The motif is groups of notes, stated over two bars, a breath in bar 3, an answer in bar 4. Strong beats land on chord tones, passing tones fill the thirds, long notes keep off the chord's semitones, and the tune sits above the chord (67..86).
- **A live set** (`music.style = 'set'`, the default): bossa, guaraldi, island, dumont, supermarket, herbs, lofi, puppetmaker, in turn. Picking a style in the app plays it at once: the next bar starts a song of that style at its first A, with the tune and the band in (`audition`). The band then leaves as play calms down. Each song ends ringing on home, takes a bar's breath, and the next one starts in a related key (F, Bb, Eb and Ab are a fourth apart).
- **Bossa**: the Rhodes on the clave, the left hand on root and fifth; drums: kick, shaker, and the rim on the clave in B. The lydian #11 and the half-step side-step are still there.
- **Guaraldi**: swung at 62%, 132 bpm, a felt grand (`piano` plus `pianoLead` for the tune), open fifths bouncing in the left hand with a half-step walk into the next root, Charleston and anticipation comps, 6/9 and 9sus chords; cocktail.c's brushes: hat on 2 and 4, a sweep, a feathered kick, the ride (ding ding-ga ding) in B.
- **Each style has its own chords** (2026-10-29, after Nikki: "a lot of songs are starting to sound very much alike"). Jazz extensions (9ths, 6/9, #11) stay only in bossa and guaraldi. Island uses triads, sixths, sus2 and an open add9. Supermarket uses plain 7ths and the V11. Herbs uses plain minor 7ths. Dumont uses a quartal minor (the So What shape, `stack = true`: voiced as written) against add9 and sus. Before this every style was lofi jazz underneath. Next on the sameness list: the Rhodes is the keys in 4 of 6 styles, every style shares one form and tune shape, and the tempos all sit at 88–132.
- The first A has no tune (the band plays the changes once), and intro and outro have no drums.
- Measured with a stub synth, 3 seeds × 6 min: rubs a minute (semitone or b9 between two sounding notes) are keys-lead ≤ 1.2, bass-keys ≤ 1, keys-keys ≤ 0.8. For comparison, loficity's are 2.7 / 4.0 / 13.9. Stem levels are balanced on renders against the Rhodes.
- The upright bass is still off (`upright = false`).

- [x] **Strum** (2026-10-29, Nikki: "more of these, in a dedicated music style, humanize it more, have options"): the `strum` style (`loop = 'strum'`), with Puppetmaker's rolled chord as the whole song.
  - **Measured on rhodes2:** E♭3 B♭3 G4 B♭4 C5 **E♭5**, a 6-note E♭6 (the transcription missed the top E♭). It's a hand's strum, +29 +23 +23 then +76 +40 ms, swelling for half a second and ringing past 3 s. Puppetmaker now uses those exact numbers.
  - **The scale:** the chord is all major pentatonic (no half-steps), which is why it floats anywhere.
  - **The style:**
    - The shape and its cousins (`lib.strum.shapes`: six, sixNine, minor7, minor11, sus), in five slow loops through a major key, a chord a bar or two at 66–76 bpm.
    - 15% of the chords float to a root outside the key (bVII, bVI, bIII), the Puppetmaker trick.
    - Every strum is humanized: gaps of 18–58 ms each ×0.6–1.5, a lag before the top two 45% of the time, a down-strum 15%, a slow harp roll at the top of the loop. A lighter re-strum of the top three comes on 3 or the and-of-2 (40%).
    - Under it: a low root, a high pentatonic answer 25% of the time (never on a floating chord), and Puppetmaker's sparse hand drums when the band plays.
  - Everything lets go at the change (rung on, these open shapes rubbed: 20–32/min before, ≤0.3 after). In the set.
  - **Ear pass (2026-10-29), Nikki:** "pretty good", "it's too random, but I like the direction". It needs a real vibe, or something built with it. Parked here; ideas for next time:
    - **Commit instead of rolling.** Every bar rolls its own dice now (strum gaps, lag, down-strum, re-strum, float, answer), which is the lesson from lofi.c we skipped. Pick the strum feel **per song** (one gap, one lag habit, one re-strum spot) and keep it. Leave only the micro-timing loose.
    - **Plan the floats.** Put the outside chord on a fixed spot in the loop (bar 4 of every 4, say), so it becomes the song's signature move rather than a surprise.
    - **A rhythm that comes back.** The strums and re-strums as a pattern seeded by its place, like the tune is elsewhere, so every A strums the same way.
    - **A form and a hook.** Intro, A and B, outro, with the planner's own tune (seeded by place, on vibes or the kalimba) over the strums, or the high answers turned into a real two-bar motif.
    - **Something to lean on.** A groove under it: a soft kick and shaker, a moving bass, or a guitar-like pattern (`INSTR_GUITAR` strumming the same shapes). Or set it inside another style as its signature: island's or lofi's B, or a song's intro.
    - **Vibes to aim at:** a K.K. Slider-ish campfire guitar, Khruangbin's slow and dry, Hiroshi Yoshimura's ambient Rhodes, Mac DeMarco's woozy chords.
    - Options to expose once it has a vibe: the voice (Rhodes, felt piano, nylon guitar, harp), strum speed, float odds and shapes.

## How to listen without the app
`luajit claudetools/render-music.lua out.wav 40 set auto 16 3` renders the real music.lua offline (args: seconds, style, rhythm, busy-from seconds, seed). `MELODY=<patch>` swaps the tune's voice, and `SOLO=piano,ride` renders a stem and prints its rms while it sounds (use that to balance a new patch). It needs `native/desound/build.sh` to have been run.

## Lessons from dreamengine (radio-arranger-lessons.md), in short
- Plan the song and the bar, then play the plan. Coin flips while playing make parts flicker instead of commit.
- Seed the tune by its place in the form. That's what makes a hook.
- Count rubs by register, part pair by part pair, before tuning by ear. A chord tone can still sit a semitone from a note the chord holds, for example the root an octave over a maj7.
- On a swung grid, lengths have to be measured on the grid. Straight 16th lengths ring into the next downbeat.
- The sound was half the gap: no tape saturation on the keys, not too dark, and balance the stems by measuring.
