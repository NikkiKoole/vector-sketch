# Music & sound ideas

status: idea

Notes to pick up now and then, with no deadline. The music lives in `src/music.lua` and the patches in `src/synth.lua`. The engine is dreamengine, through `native/desound/`.

## Where it is now
- **Planned, not rolled** (2026-10-28), after dreamengine's lofi.c (its `docs/design/radio-arranger-lessons.md` is the why). Each song gets a form (intro, A, A, B, A, outro, or intro, A, B, A, B, outro), an A and a B progression (B is its own move or A with a ii-V turnaround) and a comping taste. Every bar is planned knowing the next: the push (the next chord comped early), fills into B, the bass letting go when the next chord comes in early.
- **A tune that comes back.** The melody is seeded by its place in the form, so every A sings the same tune and B its own. The motif is groups of notes, stated over two bars, a breath in bar 3, an answer in bar 4. Strong beats land on chord tones, passing tones fill the thirds, long notes keep off the chord's semitones, and the tune sits above the chord (67..86).
- **A live set** (`music.style = 'set'`, the default): bossa, guaraldi, island, dumont, supermarket, herbs, lofi, puppetmaker, strum, satie, ambient, sesame, citypop, buymode, in turn. Picking a style in the app plays it at once: the next bar starts a song of that style at its first A, with the tune and the band in (`audition`). The band then leaves as play calms down. Each song ends ringing on home, takes a bar's breath, and the next one starts in a related key (F, Bb, Eb and Ab are a fourth apart).
- **Bossa**: the Rhodes on the clave, the left hand on root and fifth; drums: kick, shaker, and the rim on the clave in B. The lydian #11 and the half-step side-step are still there.
- **Guaraldi**: swung at 62%, 132 bpm, a felt grand (`piano` plus `pianoLead` for the tune), open fifths bouncing in the left hand with a half-step walk into the next root, Charleston and anticipation comps, 6/9 and 9sus chords; cocktail.c's brushes: hat on 2 and 4, a sweep, a feathered kick, the ride (ding ding-ga ding) in B.
- **Each style has its own chords** (2026-10-29, after Nikki: "a lot of songs are starting to sound very much alike"). Jazz extensions (9ths, 6/9, #11) stay only in bossa and guaraldi. Island uses triads, sixths, sus2 and an open add9. Supermarket uses plain 7ths and the V11. Herbs uses plain minor 7ths. Dumont uses a quartal minor (the So What shape, `stack = true`: voiced as written) against add9 and sus. Before this every style was lofi jazz underneath. Next on the sameness list: the Rhodes is the keys in 4 of 6 styles, every style shares one form and tune shape, and the tempos all sit at 88–132.
- The first A has no tune (the band plays the changes once), and intro and outro have no drums.
- Measured with a stub synth, 3 seeds × 6 min: rubs a minute (semitone or b9 between two sounding notes) are keys-lead ≤ 1.2, bass-keys ≤ 1, keys-keys ≤ 0.8. For comparison, loficity's are 2.7 / 4.0 / 13.9. Stem levels are balanced on renders against the Rhodes.
- The upright bass is still off (`upright = false`).

## Music that follows the world (iMUSE)
LucasArts' iMUSE (Monkey Island 2): the music changes where you are, on a musical boundary, not with a hard cut.

**What iMUSE actually did** (Michael Land and Peter McConnell, Monkey Island 2, 1991; US patent 5,315,057): the composer put **decision points** in the music (places where a change sounds right), and at those the game could fire **hooks**: jump to another point in the music (not the start), transpose, **turn single instruments on or off**, change the volume. Composed **transition pieces** led from one tune into the next. In the swamp only a flute plays at first, and more instruments join as you go deeper. In **Woodtick** every building had the same tune in its own arrangement, so walking in changed the band, not the song. It mostly moved instruments, it hardly ever swapped whole songs.

**Ear pass on our first version (2026-10-30), Nikki:** too slow. The old song plays to the end of its phrase, goes quiet (the bridge bar), then the new one starts: up to 5 bars of gap, no overlap. "Mix it more": instruments leaving and arriving one by one, not the whole song as a block. Maybe not from the intro. Split: the music session does the handoff and `music.layers`, the rooms session (playtime-fc) the room data, the zones and the peek.
- [x] **A staggered handoff instead of the bridge bar.** Built 2026-10-30; tried live on real door trips (overworld → bakery → back): about 2–3 bars a change, both ways. The cue lands at the next bar. In that bar the old song keeps only its light parts (keys, tune, pads, harp): the heavy ones (drums, bass, riff, guitar, horns) drop at once, and every note is clipped at the bar line so the releases fade it. The new song starts on the next downbeat in its own tempo, light first: bar 1 keys, bass, pads and the tune, bar 2 the riff and the guitar, bar 3 the drums and the horns.
- [x] **Enter where the energy is.** The new song comes in at its A, or at its B (the chorus) when the old one was in its B. Never the intro: that's for songs in the set.
- [x] **Layers: the Woodtick trick.** Built 2026-10-30, specced; not used by a room yet. `music.layers(preset)` switches which parts play on the next bar line, same song: `full`, `quiet` (no drums and no bass), `cellar` (bass and keys), `rhythm` (no tune and no keys), or a table `{ drums = false, ... }`. `music.layerPresets` lists them for the room UI. `music.cue(style, { layers = preset })` brings a new song in already arranged, and acts like `music.layers` when the style already plays. A room with style `keep` plus a preset changes only the arrangement.
- [ ] **Fade what still rings.** Pool patches (one style's own voices, since 2026-10-30) can be turned down by their slot gain without touching the next song; shared ones (rhodes, piano, kick) can only be clipped.
- [ ] **A preview for the door peek** (asked by the rooms session): `music.preview(style, gain)`, the next room quietly under this one. Needs two songs booked at once, so it comes after the handoff.
- [x] **Rooms name their music** (2026-10-30). In settings → room music: a style, or `keep`, and when to turn (`phrase` or `bar`). It's saved in the scene file (`data.music`, `src/room-music.lua`). Entering the room calls `music.cue(style, { at })`. The cue waits for the phrase end, plays a bridge bar, and starts the new style at its first A in a related key.

## How to listen without the app
In the app, settings → music lists what plays (2026-10-30): one line per part (lead, keys, guitar, horns, strings, bass, drums, ...) with the patch names, brackets on what sounds right now, the rest heard in the last 4 seconds. Use those names when talking about a voice to tweak. From the bridge: `return require('src.music').playing()`.

`luajit claudetools/render-music.lua out.wav 40 set auto 16 3` renders the real music.lua offline (args: seconds, style, rhythm, busy-from seconds, seed). `MELODY=<patch>` swaps the tune's voice, and `SOLO=piano,ride` renders a stem and prints its rms while it sounds (use that to balance a new patch). It needs `native/desound/build.sh` to have been run.

## Lessons from dreamengine (radio-arranger-lessons.md), in short
- Plan the song and the bar, then play the plan. Coin flips while playing make parts flicker instead of commit.
- Seed the tune by its place in the form. That's what makes a hook.
- Count rubs by register, part pair by part pair, before tuning by ear. A chord tone can still sit a semitone from a note the chord holds, for example the root an octave over a maj7.
- On a swung grid, lengths have to be measured on the grid. Straight 16th lengths ring into the next downbeat.
- The sound was half the gap: no tape saturation on the keys, not too dark, and balance the stems by measuring.
